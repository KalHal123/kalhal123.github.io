Why I still like gray boxes

Posted 15 Oct 2026 by Sam · 4 min read · filed under: interfaces, nostalgia

Every few years I open an old screenshot of a desktop from the mid-nineties and feel oddly calm. There is a teal background, a handful of icons, and a window that looks like a window. Nothing is trying to be a card. Nothing fades in from the side.

Everything has an edge

The best thing about the beveled look is that you can tell what is a button. It sticks out. When you press it, it goes in. That is the whole idea, and it still works better than most flat buttons I have used this year.

If a thing can be clicked, it should look like it can be clicked. If it cannot, it should stop pretending.

Menus that wait for you

A menu bar lists every single thing the program can do, in words, in one row. You do not have to guess what an icon means. You just move the mouse over File and read. Try the menu bar at the top of this window; it is made of plain CSS and nothing else.

Making your own

You only need a few ingredients:

  • a flat gray rectangle
  • one light edge and one dark edge
  • a navy title bar with white bold text
  • a lot of patience for pixel borders
.raised {
  background: #c0c0c0;
  box-shadow: inset -1px -1px #000,
              inset 1px 1px #fff;
}

That is all the magic there is. The rest is writing things down and putting them where people can find them. Thanks for reading, and sign the guestbook on your way out.
